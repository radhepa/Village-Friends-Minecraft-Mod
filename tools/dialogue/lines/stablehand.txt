# Stablehand: the stablehand's work talk, station hints and jokes, what anyone says near the village's stabled
# horses (village.horses), and how residents react to horse deeds.
# Deed kinds: stole_horse (riding or leading a village horse far from its stall), returned_horse (bringing a lost
# or stolen village horse home). {victim} is the resident who keeps the horse; a communal stable horse has no
# keeper, so most lines here need only {player}, {name} and {village}.

## work.stablehand
I mucked out every stall before breakfast. The horses watched me do it. Not one of them offered to help.
Hay in the troughs, water in the cauldron, straw on the floor. That's a stable morning.
A horse that's brushed every day trusts you. A horse that's brushed once a month tolerates you.
I top up the troughs once a day. Any more and the horses get round and lazy.
The Saddle Rack is mine. Touch the saddle if you like, but put it back how you found it.
Every horse in the stable has its own stall and its own name. I won't tell you which one's my favorite.
Destriers are big, brave and greedy. Mostly greedy.
A Palfrey's gait is so smooth you could carry a full mug of cider and not spill a drop.
Coursers are built for speed. They'd race the wind if the wind ever agreed to it.
The Rouncey is the everyday horse. Not grand, not fast, but it never lets you down.
Draft horses pull carts and plows all day and then ask for an apple like they did nothing.
The Desert Horse can go a long way on very little. I could learn something from it.
Steppe Ponies jump like they've got springs in their hooves. Short legs, big hearts.
The Fjord Horse has a mane like a broom and a temper like a summer afternoon.
Hurt horses and foals find the trough on their own. They're cleverer than people think.
A stall is a horse's home. Lead one in and it'll stay close to it from then on.
I sell papers for horses. Hand them over somewhere with room and your new horse is there to meet you.
Don't feed a horse from your palm with your fingers out. Flat hand. Trust me.
I check every hoof every morning. Stones get in, and a horse can't tell you where it hurts.
The knights ride the night watch from our stalls. I have the horses saddled before they ask.
Leather tack needs oil or it cracks. Saddles, bridles, all of it. Every week.
Mail barding is heavy. A horse wearing it needs more hay and more patience.
Plate barding is for war horses. I hope ours never need it.
A bridle lets you ride bareback, if you're brave. I recommend a saddle.
Saddlebags hold more than you'd think. More than you should carry, honestly.
Donkeys are cleverer than horses and they know it. Mules know it twice as much.
A pack saddle on a mule and you can move half a house in one trip.
Wheat for a snack, a hay bale for a feast. Golden carrots for horses who've been very, very good.
The foals are the best part of the job. And the worst part, when they get out.
I talk to the horses while I work. They're good listeners. They never interrupt.
Rain doesn't stop stable work. Horses still eat when it's wet. So do I.
Some horses love the brush. Some pretend they don't and then lean into it.
A horse that trusts you will come when you whistle. It takes time to earn that.
I can tell a horse's mood from its ears. Forward is curious. Pinned back means leave.
The barn cats sleep in the hay. The horses don't mind. I've given up minding.
Someone left the gate open once. We spent a whole afternoon chasing horses through the wheat.
When a horse is hurt I bring it hay and stay a while. Company helps more than you'd think.
The best saddle is the one that fits. A pretty saddle that pinches is worse than none.
Every morning the horses hear me coming and start stamping for breakfast. It's a nice welcome.

## station.stablehand
My Saddle Rack is where I work. Put one near a free resident and you might find yourself a stablehand.
Ride or lead a horse you own to a Horse Stall and use it. That stall is its home from then on.
Fill a Hay Trough with wheat for one serving, or a hay bale to fill it right up.
A hurt horse or a foal will eat from a trough near its stall. Keep the hay coming.
Use Horse Papers on the ground and the horse they're for comes to meet you, tame and yours.
Use an empty stall with an empty hand and it'll tell you whose it is.

## joke.job.stablehand
Why did the horse eat with its mouth open? Bad stable manners.
What do you call a horse that lives next door? A neigh-bor.
Why did the pony need a drink of water? It was a little hoarse.
Why don't horses ever win at cards? They always show their hand. Well. Their hoof.

## village.horses
The horses are restless today. Maybe they know something we don't.
I love the sound of the stable at night. All that slow, warm breathing.
That grey one in the end stall always watches me go by. I think it likes me.
You can smell the hay from here. It smells like summer, even in winter.
Our stablehand knows every horse by name. I can barely remember my neighbors'.
I rode one of the stable horses once, as a child. Fell off twice. Got back on twice.
The knights' horses are bigger than my cottage. Well. Nearly.
The foals in the stable are trying to stand up straight. It's like watching a table learn to walk.
I bring the horses an apple when nobody's looking. Don't tell the stablehand.
A village with a stable feels safer, somehow. Horses notice things before we do.

I watched you lead a village horse away. Everyone in {village} needs those horses.
I saw you take a horse from our stable. The stablehand nearly cried.
I saw you gallop off on a horse that wasn't yours. I hope you're bringing it back.
I saw you take {victim}'s horse. Right out of its stall, as if it were yours.

I heard {victim}'s horse went missing. People are saying your name.

## deed.stole_horse.self
You took my horse, {player}. I raised that horse from a foal.
My horse is gone, and everyone says you rode it away. Bring it home.
I went out to the stall this morning and it was empty. You know why.
That horse is my livelihood. I can't work without it. Please bring it back.

## deed.stole_horse.family
You took our family's horse. My {kin} has been looking at that empty stall all day.
{victim} says our horse is gone, and that you took it. We need that horse.
Our horse was part of the family. You'd better be treating it well.
We've been worried sick about our horse. You could at least bring it home.

## deed.returned_horse.seen
I saw you bring one of our horses home, {player}. Thank you.
I watched you lead a lost horse back to its stall. The stablehand will be so relieved.
I saw you bring back a horse that had gone missing. Good of you.
I saw you walk a horse all the way home to {village}. That's kindness.
I saw you bring {victim}'s horse back. It went straight to its stall like it had missed it.

## deed.returned_horse.heard
{teller} says you brought one of our lost horses home.
I heard a horse came back to the stable, and that you brought it.
Word is you found a lost horse and led it home. {village} owes you one.
Somebody told me you returned a horse you could easily have kept. That says a lot.
I heard {victim}'s horse is back in its stall, thanks to you.

## deed.returned_horse.self
You brought my horse home, {player}. I didn't think I'd see it again.
My horse is back in its stall. Thank you. I owe you more than I can say.
When I saw my horse walk back in, I nearly cried. Thank you for bringing it home.
You found my horse. I've been looking for days. Thank you, truly.

## deed.returned_horse.family
You brought our family's horse home. My {kin} hasn't stopped smiling.
{victim} says you brought our horse back. Thank you, from all of us.
Our horse is home, and we know who to thank. You're always welcome at our table.
