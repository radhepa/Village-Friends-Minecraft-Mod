# Homesteads: the Shepherd. A hill shepherd living alone at {place} (such as "Windy Fold"): a stone hut with a
# turf-and-spruce roof, a dry-stone sheepfold, a loom, a lookout rock. Gender-neutral, content in solitude, not
# lonely. Gentle, slow, wry, a little mystical about the hills. Reads clouds, knows the stars and the moon,
# spins and weaves on long evenings, keeps watch for wolves, walks down to market twice a year.
# The flock (keep these consistent): Bramble (stubborn ewe, disapproves of everything), Old Hob (the old ram),
# Thimble (the smallest, last-born lamb, she), Moss (grey, wanders off, he), Dulcie (vain, finest fleece, she),
# Pickle (eats everything, an orphan lamb hand-reared, he). The shepherd learned the trade from old Gammer Rook.
# Pools: shepherd.greet (+ .stranger .friend .morning .evening .night), shepherd.weather.*, shepherd.chat
# (+ .new .close), shepherd.past, shepherd.night, shepherd.work, shepherd.adventure, shepherd.joke, shepherd.news,
# shepherd.heart, shepherd.player.*, shepherd.held.*. Placeholders: {name} {player} {place} {village} {time}
# {weekday} {season} {moon} {item} (held.* only).

Good morning. The sheep are already up and complaining. As am I.

Good evening. Time to bring them in. Bramble will make it difficult. She always does.
Evening, {player}. The loom's waiting and the fire's lit. A quiet night ahead, I hope.

Night on the hill. Look up, {player}. That's why I live here.

## shepherd.weather.rain
Rain coming sideways off the hill. The sheep turn their backs to it. Wise creatures.
Wool keeps the wet off a sheep. Nothing keeps it off a shepherd.
I saw this rain coming yesterday in the mackerel clouds. Nobody listens to a shepherd.
Rain. Good for the grass, good for the spring, bad for my temper.
Come under the turf roof. It only leaks in one place, and I've put a pot there.

## shepherd.weather.thunder
Thunder in the hills sounds like giants moving furniture. Old Hob hates it.
Off the high ground, {player}! Lightning likes the tallest thing, and up here that's us.
Thimble's hiding behind my legs. I'd hide behind someone's legs too, if there were any going.

## shepherd.weather.snow
Snow. I'll have to dig the fold out by morning. The sheep will look at me like it's my fault.
Snow on the hills. Quiet as a held breath.
Sheep don't mind snow, their wool's thick. I've woven myself nearly the same coat.
When the snow comes, I count them every hour. A sheep under a drift makes no sound.
Look at Moss. Grey sheep, white hill. I'll be counting him by his shadow.

## shepherd.weather.clearing
There. The clouds are lifting off the ridge like a lid off a pot.
Cleared up. The whole hill smells of wet heather and second chances.
Rain's gone. Look, a rainbow over the valley. It ends in somebody else's field, as always.

## shepherd.weather.hot
Hot. The sheep are lying in the shade of the wall like spilled wool.
Too warm for the low slopes. I've moved them up near the spring.
This heat makes the stones shimmer. On days like this I sit very still and envy the lizards.

## shepherd.chat
Bramble got out again. I found her on the lookout rock, staring at the sky like she'd paid for the view.
Old Hob slept in the sun all afternoon. He's earned it. He's earned most things, at his age.
I spun a whole basket of wool today. My fingers think in thread now.
The clouds this morning were like fish scales. Rain by tomorrow. You'll see.
Pickle ate a thistle, then looked betrayed by it. He'll eat another tomorrow.
I've been weaving a blanket the colour of the hill at dusk. Grey, with a little gold.
The wind's from the west. It brings the smell of the sea, if you know how to listen with your nose.
Thimble followed me all the way to the spring and back. She thinks I'm her mother. I've stopped correcting her.
Moss wandered off again. Found him two valleys over, eating someone else's clover.
Dulcie's fleece is the finest on the hill. She knows it. She stands in the wind so it ruffles nicely.
Up here a day is long and full and nothing happens in it. That's the gift.
I sat on the lookout rock and watched a hawk for an hour. He caught nothing. Neither did I. We were both content.
I sing to the flock in the evenings. Bramble yawns, but Bramble yawns at everything.
The spring above the fold is running sweet and cold. Have a drink before you go.
Mended the fold wall today. Dry stone, no mortar. Every stone has to agree with its neighbours.
I counted them this morning: six. I count them every morning. It's never been seven, but I live in hope.
A sheep can't tell you what's wrong. You learn to read the ears.
People down in {village} talk all day and say nothing. Sheep say nothing all day and you learn everything.
The heather's coming purple on the south side. The bees will be up soon, humming like a loom.
I've been dyeing wool with onion skins. It comes out the colour of a good sunset.
Pickle got his head stuck in the feed bucket. Wore it like a helmet for an hour. Very dignified.
I watched the cloud shadows cross the valley today, like a flock moving on to new grass.
There's an old standing stone past the ridge. I leave it a tuft of wool every season. For luck. Or for manners.
The hills aren't empty. They're full. You just have to be quiet enough to notice.
Old Hob butted the fold gate this morning. Not to get out. Just to remind it who's in charge.
I talk to the sheep more than to people. The sheep have better manners and worse breath.
Long day. Good day. The two often go together up here.
I wove a strip of blue into the new cloak. Just one. A little bit of sky to take about with me.
Bramble and I have an understanding. I pretend to be in charge, and she lets me.
The wind dropped at noon. It was so quiet I could hear the grass growing. Or Pickle eating it.
It's {weekday}. Down below, that means something. Up here it's another day of clouds.
The lambs came late this year. Thimble came last and smallest, and she's my favourite. Don't tell the others.
Wool, water, grass and sky. You'd be surprised how far those four go.
Today I followed a beetle along the wall for a while. It knew exactly where it was going. I admired that.
My loom has a voice. Clack, hush, clack. Better company than most people I met at market.
I patched the turf roof. Now the grass grows on my house too. Pickle keeps eyeing it.
The sheep know the weather before I do, and I know it before anyone else. There's an order to these things.

## shepherd.chat.close
People think I'm lonely up here. I'm not. Lonely was standing in a crowded room in {village}.
Some nights I worry I'll grow old up here and nobody will know. Then I think: the sheep will know. That helps more than it should.
When Old Hob goes, and he'll go before long, I don't know what I'll do. He's been with me longer than anyone.
I've never told anyone this, but I'm afraid of the dark under trees. Not the open dark. The hills, I trust.
I had a brother once. We don't speak. I weave him a scarf every winter and never send it. There's a chest full of them now.
You're the only one I'd let shear Dulcie. That's the highest honour this hill has to give.
I think the hills are alive, in a slow way. I think they know who loves them. Does that sound foolish? Good. Only to you, then.
When my time comes, I'd like to be buried by the lookout rock, facing east. I've told the sheep, but they'll forget.
I was a loud child, you know. It took a lot of years on this hill to get this quiet.
I'm glad you come up here, {player}. I didn't know I'd been waiting for someone until you started coming.
Some market days I stand at the edge of the square and think about staying. Then a bell rings and I'm back up the hill by dark.
I keep a lantern in the window on stormy nights. Not for me. In case anyone's lost on the hill. Nobody ever has been. I keep it lit anyway.
The flock are my family. I know how that sounds. It isn't sad. They're very good family.

## shepherd.past
I grew up in {village}, the third of five. Nobody noticed when I took the old crook off the wall and walked uphill.
The old shepherd who taught me was called Gammer Rook. She knew every star and never once used its proper name.
My first winter here I lost two sheep to the snow. I learned to count every hour after that.
Gammer Rook gave me Old Hob when he was a lamb. Said he'd outlive us both. He's halfway there.
I built the fold wall myself, stone by stone, one long summer. Every stone has a story. Most of the stories are "ouch".
After Gammer Rook died, I tried living down in {village}. I lasted one season. The bells rang right through my head.
Bramble was born in a hailstorm. She's been cross about it ever since.
My grandmother taught me to weave. She said a loom teaches you patience or it teaches you to swear. I learned both.
The year the wolves came, I slept in the fold with my crook across my knees for a whole month.
My first night alone up here, I didn't sleep at all. The stars kept me company. They still do.
When I was small I thought the hills were sleeping giants. I'm older now. I'm less sure they aren't.
Pickle was an orphan lamb. I fed him from a cloth soaked in milk. He's been hungry ever since.
I walked to the coast once, years ago, and saw the sea. It was like the hills, only restless. I came home.

## shepherd.night

It's a {moon} tonight. Gammer Rook could read a month's weather from that. I can read about a week.
I'll sit up a while. Wolves were calling over the ridge at dusk.
The stars all wheel round the still one. Everything up there has its fold, same as down here.
There's the {moon}, or what shows of it. Gammer Rook said the moon's a shepherd too, counting the stars home.
Hush. Listen to them breathing in the fold. Six little clouds of breath. That's the sound of all being well.
Some nights I see lights moving on the far ridge. Pillagers, maybe. Or something older. I keep the fire low.
The Plough's tipping over the ridge. That means it's late. That means you should be sleeping, and so should I.
I don't need a lantern up here. The sky's lantern enough, most nights.
Night watch is long, but it's never empty. There's always another star to learn.

## shepherd.work
Shearing's in early summer. Dulcie stands still for it. Bramble makes it a wrestling match.
I move the flock to new grass every few days. Keep them moving and the hill keeps giving.
Spinning is thinking with your hands. The thread gets smoother as the mind does.
A good crook is half the job. Mine's older than I am. It's hooked more sheep out of brambles than I can count, including a Bramble.
The fold wall needs mending after every frost. Stones heave in the cold, like old bones.
Every week I check each foot, each eye, each ear. Sheep hide it when they're poorly. You have to look.
A blanket takes me most of a month of evenings to weave. Selling it takes a moment, and I always regret it.
I take the wool down to market twice a year, spring and autumn. Both times I'm home before dark if I can help it.
At lambing time I barely sleep. Every cry might be the one that needs you.
Keeping sheep is mostly keeping watch. The rest is walking and worrying.
I wash the fleeces in the spring and lay them out on the heather to dry. The hill looks snowed on in summer.
Wolves are the worst of it. On bad nights I keep a fire at the fold gate and sing loud. Wolves don't like singing. Neither does Bramble.
I've dyed wool with heather, onion, lichen and walnut. The hills give you every colour if you ask nicely.
The loom's for the long evenings. The crook's for the long days. The hut's for whatever's left over.
Pickle has to be fed apart from the others or he eats everyone's share. Every flock has one.
A shepherd's job is simple: know where every sheep is, always. It's the "always" that's hard.

## shepherd.adventure
If you're crossing high ground, watch the clouds on the peaks. When they come down, you go down.
I went to the sea once. That was enough adventure for one life.
Take wool socks. Two pairs. Adventurers always forget their feet.
If you're lost in the hills, follow the water downhill. It always knows where the people are.
Wolves follow more than they chase. If you hear them behind you, keep walking, and keep a fire at night.
You go and see the world, {player}. Then come up and tell me about it. That's my favourite way to travel.
Mind the pillagers' towers. On clear days I can see their banners from the lookout rock. I keep well wide.
Every road you walk, a sheep probably walked it first. They're very good at finding the easy way.
There's a cave under the eastern crag. I've never gone in. Some places are better left as mysteries.

## shepherd.joke

I tried counting sheep to get to sleep last night. I only have six. I was wide awake by seven.
Bramble walked into the hut once, looked at me, looked at the loom, and walked out again. I've never felt so judged.
I told Old Hob a joke this morning. He didn't laugh. He's heard it before. He's heard all of them before.
Moss isn't lost. Moss is exploring. Moss has been exploring for three days now.
A wolf once dressed up as a sheep to sneak into my fold. I knew straight away. Too quiet. My sheep never stop talking.
Where does a sheep go for a trim? The baa-baa's, of course. Bramble won't go. Bramble cuts her own.
What do you get if you cross a sheep with a thundercloud? A very wet blanket. No? The sheep didn't laugh either.
Why did the ram run off the cliff? He didn't see the ewe-turn. I'm sorry. The hills make you do it.

## shepherd.heart
