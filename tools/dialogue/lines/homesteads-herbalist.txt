## herbalist.weather.rain
Rain! The moss on my roof is having the time of its life.
Let it pour. The garden's been sulking for a week; this will cheer it right up.
Wet weather brings out the mushrooms and the slugs. One of those I'm pleased to see.
Rain on the roof and something on the boil. There are worse ways to spend a day.
Come under the eaves. Nettles gathered in the rain sting less, but you're not a nettle.

## herbalist.weather.thunder
Folk say lightning once struck a farmer out by the swamp and they woke up a witch. Folk say a lot of things about me, too.
Thunder sours milk and sweetens elderflower. Or so Sorrel swore. I've never managed to prove either.
Stay in till it passes. The cauldron hates thunder; it rattles its lid like it wants to answer back.
Storms bring the night callers early. Fear makes people remember their coughs.

## herbalist.weather.snow
Snow. The garden's asleep, and so should I be. Instead I'm grinding dried yarrow by the fire.
Everything's under white. Good. The bees are snug, and the gossip from the village can't climb the hill.
Snow's a blanket for the roots. Under it, everything's quietly planning for spring.

## herbalist.weather.clearing
There, it's lifting. Go and smell the garden now; it won't smell this good again till the next rain.
Sky's clearing. The bees will be out in a moment, cross about the delay.
The clouds are breaking. Wet petals bruise, so I'll wait an hour before I pick. You can wait with me.

## herbalist.weather.hot
Hot enough to dry herbs on the doorstep. Saves me climbing to the rafters.
Too warm for the cauldron. Too warm for anything but sitting in the shade and judging the bees.
Drink something. In this heat a body wilts faster than lettuce, and it's harder to revive.

## herbalist.chat
Folk in {village} call me a witch behind my back. To my face they call me "please" and "it's only a little cough".
Never eat a mushroom somebody else picked unless you'd trust them with your life. That's not a saying. That's a rule.
A bee never visits the same flower twice in a morning. Don't go back for more from what's already given you everything.
Pick leaves by the waxing moon and roots by the waning. Does it matter? It matters to me, and I'm the one picking.
Old saying: "Where nettles grow, good soil lies below." Same's true of some people.
Foxglove is beautiful and it will stop your heart. I grow it at the back of the garden so I always know exactly where it is.
Dandelions are a weed to everyone but me. Leaves for salad, root for the liver, seeds for wishing. Nothing wasted.
The trouble with being needed is that people resent you for it.
Yarrow stops bleeding. Honey keeps a wound clean. A kind word does neither, but it doesn't hurt to have one handy.
My roof's more moss than slate. I keep it that way. It holds the warmth, and the wrens love it.
Lily of the valley smells like heaven and is poison from root to bell. Never eat it. Never let a child near it.
A saying of mine: "Sweet berries, sour thorns, and the bush apologises for neither."
The village buys honey from me at market and swears it came from someone else's hive. My bees know their own work.
Glow berries are the only lamp that needs no oil. I've strung them in the cellar. It's like living under a small, soft sky.
Every remedy is half plant and half listening. Most people can't get the listening anywhere else.
Poppies for sleep, but only a little. Too much and you'll sleep through things you'd rather have been awake for.
Elderflower in spring, elderberries in autumn. The same tree gives you a cordial and a cough syrup. Generous thing.
Want to know whether a stranger's honest? Offer them nettle tea. Liars always say they love it.
I talk to the plants. Not because they listen. Because nobody else up here does.
Cornflowers for tired eyes. I steep them for the scribes in {village}. They squint at me less, after.
Oxeye daisies grow where nobody wants them, and they bloom anyway. I've taken a lesson from that.
The bees and I have an arrangement. I don't take more than I need, and they don't sting more than I deserve.
"Moss grows on the north side," they say. Moss grows wherever it likes. Navigate by the stars, or by sense.
Alliums keep the pests off the roses. Some people are like alliums. They smell a bit odd and the garden's better for them.
Azure bluets are small and plain, and they ease a headache better than anything showy. Remember that.
Brown mushrooms like the dark, the damp, and being left alone. I've always felt we understood each other.
Someone left a basket of eggs at my gate last week. No note. That's how the village says thank you.
A garden is just a long argument with the weather that you occasionally win.
Don't mistake cryptic for wise. I'm cryptic because it makes people leave sooner.
The moon pulls at the water, and every leaf is mostly water. So yes, the moon has opinions about my garden.
Honey never spoils. I've a jar older than some of my visitors. Still sweet.
Sorrel used to say, "Heal the body, then mind your tongue about the rest." I'm still learning the second half.
I hang a sprig of yarrow over the door. Not for luck. It's just where I keep the yarrow.
You'd be amazed what a cup of something warm and a closed door can cure.
"A weed is only a flower nobody has bothered to learn." My own saying. You may borrow it.
If the bees are cross, rain's coming. If I'm cross, someone from the village has been gossiping again.
Zombies leave my garden alone. I'd like to say it's the herbs. It's probably the compost heap.
Moss on the roof, mushrooms in the cellar, bees in the hive. I'm never as alone as people think.
Real witches live in swamp huts and throw bottles and cackle. I've never cackled in my life. Well. Once. It was a good joke.
I could live in {village} if I wanted to. I don't want to. The quiet up here is a remedy too.

## herbalist.chat.close
The one who taught me was called Sorrel. Lived in this very cottage. Knew every plant by name and every person by their cough.
Sorrel used to say the cottage chose who lived in it. When Sorrel died, I came up the path with my bag and the door wasn't locked. I took that as an answer.
I nursed a whole street through a fever one winter. One old man didn't pull through, and he's the only one they remember.
Why do I live apart? A village needs someone to blame and someone to thank. It's easier for them if I'm far enough away to be both.
It costs something, living like this. Nobody brings soup when I'm poorly. I bring it to myself, and I'm a terrible patient.
Some nights I sit on the step and watch the lanterns in {village}, and I think about walking down. Then I think about the faces, and I put the kettle on instead.
Sorrel's hands smelled of yarrow till the very end. Mine are starting to. I didn't expect to be glad of it.
I keep a book of everyone who's come to my door. Not names; just what they needed. It's a thick book, {player}. It's a lonely book.
You're the first person in years who's come up the path without needing anything. Do you know what a gift that is?
I could have been a healer in a big town, with a sign over the door and people who said good morning. I chose the bees. Most days I'd choose them again.
Sorrel's buried under the elder tree. I talk to that tree more than I talk to people. Don't look at me like that; you would too.
The worst part isn't being called a witch. It's someone I sat up all night with crossing the street at market so as not to be seen near me.
If anything happens to me, the recipes are in the tin behind the third jar. You'd know who to give them to. I trust that you would.

## herbalist.past
I learned my letters from seed packets and my sums from counting bees. Not the usual schooling.
My first remedy was for my own nettle stings. I was seven and furious, and I've been mixing things ever since.
When I was young I wanted to be a sailor. Then I got seasick on a duck pond. Plants don't rock.
I came to this cottage as an apprentice with one bag and a great many opinions. The opinions got sorted out first.
Sorrel made me weed the garden for a whole year before I was allowed near a pot. I was so cross. I understand now.
The first night someone knocked for a remedy, I was so nervous I gave them the wrong jar. It worked anyway. Faith's strong medicine.
I once spent a whole summer learning the mushrooms of this wood. Don't ask me about them; we'd be here till winter.
There was a time I lived in {village}. Long ago. I had a window box and three friends. I still have the window box.
My mother said I'd grow up strange. She was right, but she said it fondly, and I've held on to that.
Years back a wolf limped into the garden and lay down by the hive and wouldn't leave. I splinted its leg. It visited every winter after, until one winter it didn't.
The first hive I kept swarmed within a week and left me for a hollow oak. Bees, like people, won't be kept against their will.
Before the beehive, before the cauldron, I had a mortar and a mean streak. The mortar's still here. The mean streak's mellowed.
I once walked three days to a market town for a single pot of seeds. Half of them came up foxglove. I've never been so annoyed or so careful.
