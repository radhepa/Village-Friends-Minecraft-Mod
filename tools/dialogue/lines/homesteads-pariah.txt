# Homesteads: the Pariah, who lives alone at a once-fine timber house going to seed, far from any village.
# Cast out of a village years ago: once a councillor, asked for judgement and advice, with friends, a seat of honour
# at the tavern and their name on the bell rope. Then an accusation, a vote, and friends who looked at their boots.
# What they were accused of is never spelled out: a lie, half a lie, or something they'd do again. Never resolved.
# Voice: mostly scorn, pride and dry wit. Rare cracks of loneliness. Warmer (but still prickly) in .friend and .close.
# Old names, used consistently: Aldous (oldest friend, kept bees, studied his boots at the vote), Merrit (council
# rival, took the seat), Wenna (tavern keeper who kept their chair by the hearth), Tobin (the clerk they trained, who
# cast the last vote). The one they nearly married stays unnamed.
# Keys: pariah.greet, pariah.greet.stranger, pariah.greet.friend, pariah.greet.morning/evening/night,
# pariah.weather.rain/thunder/snow/clearing/hot, pariah.chat, pariah.chat.new, pariah.chat.close, pariah.past,
# pariah.night, pariah.work, pariah.adventure, pariah.joke, pariah.news, pariah.heart, pariah.player.hurt/hungry/dark/wet,
# pariah.held.sword/flower/food/emerald.
# Placeholders: {place} (their homestead), {village} (nearest village, or "the village"), {player}, {moon}, {weekday},
# and {item} in held.* only. Gender-neutral throughout.

Evening. The lanterns are lit down there. I can count them from here. I do, most nights.

## pariah.weather.rain
Rain again. It comes in through the cracked window and I let it. We understand each other.
Wet weather. At least nobody expects me at market in this.
Listen to it on the roof. The only thing that visits me without being asked.
Rain washes {village} clean, they used to say. It hasn't managed yet.
Rain's good for the garden. The weeds, mostly. They're the only things that thrive up here.

## pariah.weather.thunder
Thunder. The sky shouting at everyone at once. I used to do that in council. Very satisfying.
Let it storm. This house has stood worse. So have I.
Hear that? That's the only applause I get these days.
A storm like this, the whole of {village} will be huddled together. I'll be huddled with myself. Better company.

## pariah.weather.snow
Snow. Everything looks clean and forgiven. Don't be fooled.
Snowed in again. As if I needed another excuse to see nobody.
My first winter out here, someone left firewood at my gate. I never found out who. I never thanked them. Snow always reminds me.
Snow on the fence posts. It hides the rot nicely. I could use a coat of it myself.

## pariah.weather.clearing
Rain's stopped. The world goes back to pretending it never cried.
There. The sky's done sulking. I could learn from it, I'm told. I won't.
Clearing up. The puddles show the house upside down. It looks better that way.

## pariah.weather.hot
Hot enough to wilt a council. Not that mine needed much help.
This heat. The garden's sulking, the well's low and I'm in no mood. Nothing new, then.
Too hot to hold a grudge. Almost. I'm managing.

## pariah.past
Aldous and I learned our letters on the same slate. Forty years of friendship, and at the vote he studied his boots like they owed him money.
Merrit always wanted my seat on the council. He has it now. I hope it pinches.
My name was on the bell rope. Carved in, right under the founder's. I wonder if they sanded it off, or if they just pull past it.
People used to stop me in the street. Not to chat. To ask. Should we dig the well here, should we let the stranger stay. I'd tell them, and they'd do it.
Wenna kept my chair by the tavern hearth for twenty years. Nobody else dared sit in it. I hear Merrit sits there now. I hope it wobbles.
Tobin was a scrawny boy when I took him on as clerk. I taught him to read a ledger and stand up straight. He stood up very straight indeed to cast the last vote.
The day of the vote it rained. Everyone remembers the rain. Nobody remembers who spoke for me, because nobody did.
I'll tell you what I was accused of when you tell me what you'd have done in my place. No? Then we're square.
Was it true? Half of it. The wrong half. The half they could prove.
It was a lie. A good one. Merrit always did tell a lie well. It's the only thing he does well.
I'd do it again. Don't ask me what. Just know I'd do it again, and sleep fine after.
When I walked down the high street that last day, nobody threw anything. That was worse, somehow. They just closed their shutters, one after another, like a row of eyelids.
Aldous came to see me once, the first spring. Stood at the gate, didn't come in. Said he was sorry. I told him sorry was for the vote, and he'd missed it.
Eleven years on the council. I settled boundaries, fed the poor through the hard winter, faced down a pillager captain with a lantern and bad manners. One vote, and none of it counted.
Respect isn't loud. That's what nobody tells you. It's quiet. A nod. A door held. You only hear it when it stops.
I had friends, you know. Real ones. We used to sit up past midnight at Wenna's, arguing about the harvest like it was a war.
Merrit sent me a letter after. Very gracious. Wishing me well in my new life. I used it to light the stove. It burned beautifully.
Tobin's on the council now, I hear. Good. He'll learn what it costs. Everybody learns.
Aldous kept bees. He brought me honey every autumn. I still catch myself watching the road around harvest.
They gave me three days to pack. I needed one. It turns out you can fit a whole respected life into one cart, if you leave out the people.
I used to stand at the front of the hall when the lanterns were lit, and the room would go quiet before I'd said a word. You don't forget that. You try.
I'm told Wenna still lays a cloth on my old table at the harvest feast. She says it's habit. I say it's guilt. Probably it's both.

## pariah.night
Can't sleep. Never can, under a clear sky. All those stars watching, and not one of them gets a vote.
Look at the {moon}. Thrown out of the sky every morning, and back every night like it owns the place. I respect that.
From the ridge I can see the lanterns of {village}. I tell myself I'm counting them to see who's burning oil they can't afford.
The stars don't care who you were. It's the most restful thing about them.
I lie awake and argue the vote again. I win, every night. Then the sun comes up.
Late, isn't it. The hour when everyone tells the truth, or goes to bed to avoid it.
Some nights I say their names out loud. Aldous. Wenna. Tobin. Just to check I still can. Never Merrit. Merrit I save for the daytime, with feeling.

## pariah.work
Work? I keep this house standing. Nobody visits it, but it stands. That's spite, and spite is a trade.
I used to weigh disputes over boundaries and wells and stolen hens. Now I weigh firewood. The firewood never appeals.
I keep a ledger. Every slight, every turned back, every shutter closed. It's very well kept. I was always good with ledgers.
I tend the garden. The roses have thorns and the herbs are bitter. I grow what suits me.
Chopping wood is good work. Every log has a face, if you're imaginative. I'm very imaginative.
I mend things. The fence, the roof, the chair legs. If you can't fix what happened, you fix what's in front of you.
Once I told a whole village when to plant. Now I plant my own beans, and I'm right about them every single year.
I copy out my old council notes. Every judgement I made. So someone, someday, can see I was right more often than they were.
Busy? I'm always busy. Being disliked properly takes upkeep.
I sell eggs to the peddler. He cheats me, and I let him think he's getting away with it. It keeps me in practice.
I hauled stones up from the creek today to fix the wall. My back aches and my mind is clear. Fair trade.
They took my seat, my name, my friends. They didn't take my hands. I can still build a better wall than anyone in {village}.
What did I do for a living? I listened. Then I decided. Then people did as I said. It was a fine job, until they decided about me.
I patch the roof before every storm. Not because anyone would mourn the house. Because I won't give them the satisfaction.
My work these days is mostly remembering. It's harder than it sounds, keeping every grievance polished.
I keep bees now. Aldous taught me, years ago. They sting when you're careless. Fair creatures.

## pariah.adventure

Heroes. Every one of them comes home expecting a feast. I've seen how fast a feast becomes a trial.
Go off slaying things and they'll sing songs about you. Then one day they'll sing something else. Trust me.
I don't need the world. The world made that very clear, and I've returned the favour.
The road out of {village} is four days' walk if you're sad, two if you're angry. I made it in two.
You want treasure? Go on. Bring it back to a village and see how many friends it buys you. Then see how long they stay bought.
I faced down pillagers once, when I had a village behind me. They gave me a cup at the feast for it. They took it back at the vote.
Adventure is for people with somewhere to come home to. I've got {place}. That'll do. It'll have to.
Don't tell me about mountains and far seas. I've seen enough of the world from the wrong side of a closed door.
