# Deeds: what residents say the first time they talk to you after learning what you did in their village.
# Kind pools: deed.<kind>.seen (they watched it), deed.<kind>.heard (someone told them), deed.<kind>.self (it was done
# to them or for them), deed.<kind>.family (it was done to or for their partner or family).
# Good kinds: raid_defended, raid_won, revived, bandaged, saved_from_monster, rescued_companion, notice_answered,
# birthday_gift, pet_kindness. Bad kinds: hit_resident, knocked_out_resident, killed_resident, hit_golem, killed_golem,
# hurt_pet, killed_pet, broke_home, stole.
# Personality pools: deed.<good|bad>.<seen|heard>.<personality>, generic enough for any deed of that sort.
# Children: baby.deed.<good|bad>.<seen|heard|family>, baby.deed.<kind>.self for the kinds a child lives through.
# Apologies: deed.apology.accept, deed.apology.cool (and .child of each), deed.apology.remembered.
# Kept memories for the Journal: deed.kept.<kind>.self and .family (revived, saved_from_monster, rescued_companion).
# The "Unwelcome" title (standing below Newcomer): greet.unwelcome, greet.unwelcome.<personality>, baby.greet.unwelcome,
# chat.unwelcome, notice.unwelcome.
# Placeholders: {victim} the involved resident's first name (for pet deeds, the pet's owner), {kin} the speaker's family
# word for them in lower case ("sister", "husband"), {teller} who told the speaker, {house} a house name ("The Ashford
# House"). Raids and golems involve no resident, so their pools never use {victim}. A line whose placeholder can't be
# filled is skipped, so every pool keeps plenty of lines that need nothing but {player}, {name} and {village}.
# Lines never describe more than the placeholders say: one line has to fit one deed or several merged together.
